SSA Announces 2026 Bathroom Safety Assistance for Seniors 65+

By Sarah Williams, Aging & Benefits Correspondent Updated 10:43 AM EST 6 min read

Quick facts

What seniors should know

  • 1 in 4 adults age 65+ falls each year (CDC)
  • ~80% of senior falls happen in the bathroom
  • SSI recipients typically meet the income test automatically
  • Average approved upgrade in 2025: $8,500–$15,000
Check my eligibility

The Social Security Administration (SSA), working alongside HUD and state Medicaid agencies, has confirmed a 2026 initiative making it easier for seniors 65 and older to upgrade aging bathrooms to safer, ADA-compliant standards — in many cases at little or no out-of-pocket cost.

According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, roughly one in four adults age 65 and older falls every year, and the bathroom remains the single most common location for those falls. Federal and state agencies have responded by expanding three of the country's largest home-safety repair programs for the 2026 fiscal year.

Eligible homeowners aged 65 and older — including those who receive Supplemental Security Income (SSI) from the Social Security Administration — can now apply for assistance through programs administered by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD Section 504 Home Repair), USDA Rural Development, and state Medicaid Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waiver programs. Many states also coordinate funding through their local Area Agency on Aging under the Older Americans Act.

Commonly approved upgrades include:
  • Step-free, ADA-compliant walk-in showers with bench seating
  • ASTM-rated grab bars and reinforced safety rails
  • Comfort-height (ADA) toilets and accessible vanities
  • Lever-handle faucets and anti-scald valves
  • Anti-slip flooring and improved task lighting

Who qualifies in 2026

Most approved applicants are 65 or older, own and live in their primary residence, and either receive SSI, qualify for their state's Medicaid HCBS waiver, or fall within HUD's "very low-income" limits for their county. Disabled adults under 65 who receive Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) may also qualify, and veterans can apply through the separate VA Home Improvements and Structural Alterations (HISA) grant.

Program reviewers note that homes built before 1995 are especially likely to be approved, since older fixtures typically don't meet current accessibility standards under the Americans with Disabilities Act.

How seniors are applying

Most approved homeowners begin with a short eligibility screening that confirms age, residence, and household income or benefit status. Applicants who already receive SSI, Medicaid, or VA benefits are generally pre-qualified on income and only need to confirm a few basic details about their home. The screening typically takes about two minutes online.
